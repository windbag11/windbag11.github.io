atop the hot air balloon

It’s like a pressure from under my skull, pinpointing every corner in this dome. It’s like being me is not even like being a person, like being entirely, wholly that person, but it’s like a sensation. A sensation that I may choose to turn on or off whenever I please. Like the world I enter when I choose to inhabit this body is but one of many doors that I may choose to enter in this hallway, in the one of many floors of dimensions that I may choose to preside over. I feel like a god, like a higher (literally and figuratively) being than every other soul on this planet, the wisest of all these humans that keep trying to work on themselves and make mistakes and learn from them or whatever, all trying to attain this highest level of personnage, which is actually me. I keep having to remind myself that I have a physical state and that I have to remember to go intentionally tap back into what being me feels like. And what it’s like at the moment is like dancing violet neon cyberpunk rhombuses flashing across the back wall of my mind. With a peculiar satisfying sensation that feels like that first millisecond of the start of relief when you itch a mosquito bite, like not that feeling of euphoria when the itch is satisfied, but the moment just before that.

This is what I wrote up until I accidentally fell asleep after my pre-bedtime joint a couple days ago, and honestly, every time I get high on my own, I feel echoes of this same sense of detachment. The previous time I felt so disconnected from my outer body that looking into the mirror felt like I was recalling a past memory of what being me felt like and not that I was me at that moment. And that any physical action I did like changing into pj’s or brushing my teeth or lighting incense was on autopilot while my mind was running full marathons such that I could have been doing everything in 2x speed or have been taking thirty second pauses between every action, and I would not have realised. What it felt like was that I was a voyeur crouching in that tunnel in Being John Malkovich, observing through the tunnel-like view of whatever my eyes were seeing, but not actually being Malkovich.

Depersonalisation is a state of detachment from oneself while derealisation is detachment from the outside world. This state can be one that originates from an attempt at psychological defence, or be triggered by use of weed, hallucinogenics, opioids etc., (actually a major drug for treatment called naxolone is one used for opioid overdoses), or just a mental disorder, and can range from hours, days, months, upto years or chronic. Psychological defense was what Freud had attributed this to, himself apparently going through this when losing his father, and also what his patient, the Wolf Man, went through due to his ‘fear of wolves’ from his dream of seeing white wolves sitting on a big walnut tree outside his window. It possibly originates from some deep rooted trauma that causes your mind to detach itself from real life as a protective mechanism. But what about the case when you don’t have a single source of trauma, at least that you can remember, from where this started? I feel this explanation is quite reductive, as naturally cautious people don’t necessarily have a singular situation from where their need of caution originates. It could potentially be a series of small events or sentiment that builds up their guard, or just from their upbringing like how their parents or their childhood painted their foundational perception of the world and its nature.

Something that also happens, at least to me, is the distortion of time. From quite early on I’ve realised that my perception of time is very skewed. I sit staring at a wall zoned out for what seems like five minutes but as it turns out, forty five minutes have passed. On the flip side, what seems to be an eternity at times ends up being a couple of minutes. I have a period of two years from which I have no memories, despite me bring about twelve to fourteen years old, thus quite a sentient age, and this period, as a present twenty year old, seems quite large. And I understand that the passing of time is a sensation that is completely dependent on how conscious the mind is at that moment, where when you have a lot of thoughts running simultaneously, as the frequency of the thoughts occuring is your only frame of reference for how time is passing, time in the real world can feel so distorted. Especially now, as I learn carnatic vocals which is built on a very rigid and defined framework of rhythm and time keeping, I realise that my sense of time keeping is so poor, because even when I use a metronome, the pauses between two ticks of the constant metronome can range from feeling really fast to taking mindnumbingly long. And this range is completely dependent on how much I’m thinking at that brief pause, whether it be keeping track of which finger the next beat should be on, validating that I’m using the correct finger and that the alankaram will end on the correct beat, or thinking about how the next swara will sound, which type of gamakam I need to be singing, making sure my singing is not out of tune, or simply just glancing at my teacher’s face for some form of feedback. This might be the curse of someone who thinks a lot or is hyperactive in their mind, but it really makes me wonder how other people’s minds work, especially those who can keep time in their mind as accurately as to that of reality. Perhaps it comes from practice, or a less turbulent mind, or the deliberate controlling of the inner cogs turning, which I haven’t quite mastered. But anyways, this feeling is amplified a thousand times when I’m high, where the frequency of my thoughts being my frame of reference is even more utterly isolated from outside factors, such that I physically cannot distinguish if I’m brushing my teeth in slow motion or at 3x the speed. And then I think even more about if I’m brushing at a normal speed, and this thinking distorts my sense of time even more, and it’s a cyclical spiral that descends into downright dread and confusion and a loss of control.

Anyhow, this also makes me question how I experience the external world outside of being high. Even when sober, having a hyperactive mind makes me a bit detached from real life interactions I have in real time. In a meta sort of way, even when conversing normally, I simultaneously evaluate the present moment and conversation almost from a third person perspective, and while I agree that everyone does this to a degree, I really question if this level of self awareness is common to everyone. This frankly might just be me overthinking something that is natural and quite simple to most people, but the way I explained this to a friend when on shrooms was using the analogy of a hot air balloon. Let’s say that everyone has their internal world, which I call their flora and fauna, and this internal world is something I like to visualise as almost a Studio Ghibli scene, such as in My Neighbour Totoro. A green grassy meadow with gushing streams and shady nooks under looming banyan trees and scattered patches of daisies and clovers. Or it could be a completely different scene for someone else. But anyhow, each person has their little flora and fauna, and when they have a conversation with someone, they are inviting them and showing them around a portion of their flora and fauna, be it just the outskirts, or maybe a lot deeper in, depending on the person. And so when I have a conversation with someone, I’m on the ground, showing them around a bit of my own flora and fauna (how many times can I say this in a paragraph). But at the same time, I’m also on a hot air balloon looking at this scene from above, watching and judging myself on how I’m showing them around, guessing what they are seeing and what they take from this, and so on. And this hot air balloon is somewhere I’ve sat in and stared down from for so long now that it has become my permanent residence. I have no control over where this hot air balloon is drifting, and I can slowly see myself drift further and further, bit by bit, and this creates such an ultimate, deep-seated fear in me, and I don’t know why, and not knowing why makes me drift even further. I don’t know where this balloon is going, or if it is going somewhere from where I can never return, somewhere no one can reach me or even see me. And I don’t know what exactly I’m fearing, if it is the lack of control, or if it’s the fear of never being reached, never understood, or something even deeper into that. I really just don’t know, and I don’t know how not knowing is making me feel. It’s a bit fucked.

I also just wonder if some of these feelings are just fundamental to what being me entails. If I classify this state of being as what being me feels like, it’s so insane to think that the way other people experience themselves can be so fundamentally different from how I experience myself. I think about this when it comes to experiencing sound, where everyone technically refer to the same thing when saying they hear something, but the manner in which each of us experience sound can be so fundamentally different and no one would ever know. Kind of like the sentiment that you and I could point to something coloured red and call it red, but what the two of us are seeing could be completely different colours and we would never know. Kind of like this sentiment but go even deeper, the way that we experience sight, sound, smell, taste could feel completely different, touching different spots in our mind, and we would never know. Like the manner in which our mind perceives the signals sent to it by our organs could be different. Like the sensation of having a sensation could be different. This honestly makes me wonder whether anyone has ever experienced the same fundamental sensation as another person, it’s a bit weird to think about. But anyhow, I also question if the current state that I keep questioning is just something I have to accept as what having my personality is. We have so many adjectives to describe people and their behaviours, be it ‘perceptive’, ‘absent-minded’, ‘clearheaded’ and so on; to what extent is the state of being that I am questioning to such extent just a basic quality that can be summed up by one such adjective to describe me, that other people accept so readily when describing themselves? I don’t ever remember being any other way, do I just accept it and move on? Or even the depersonalisation thing, is this just how I am fated to experience life atop the hot air balloon and am I asking too many questions, especially if I don’t know any other way or if other people experience the exact same thing but don’t question it?

Too many questions honestly, and at some point I do wonder if I’m going to question myself into a hole that I will never get out of. Honestly this is exactly what I mean by wishing for a life as a mindless little housefly, with only one intention and a consistent goal for the next minute of its life. Self awareness might be a unique quality only granted to human beings, but I do wonder just how deep this hole, or how high this hot air balloon, can go, up until it reaches the brink of utter insanity.
