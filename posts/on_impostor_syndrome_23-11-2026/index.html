on imposter syndrome

Stereotype threat is the phenomenon in which the fear of proving certain stereotypes about your social group correct actually impacts your performance negatively. Steele and Aronson in 1995 performed an experiment subjecting black and white people to a verbal test, where one group was told that this test was diagnostic of their intellectual ability, while another group was not. The black participants in the diagnostic test condition performed worse than the white participants, compared to those in the non-diagnostic condition, and the researchers hypothesised that being made aware that this test evaluated their intellectual ability made black participants aware of the stereotype of lower intellect among black people, and this fear of conforming to this stereotype ultimately made them underperform, compared to those who were not made aware of the diagnostic quality of the test, and thus the presence of this possible judgement at all.

I think any minority in any field experiences a large degree of stereotype threat, where, as a minority you constantly feel like you yourself are representative of your entire social group, your people. With this heavy burden that falls on your frail shoulders, the pressure of making sure you always show your best and prove these prejudices wrong, because you represent something much bigger than just yourself, this debilitating weight makes you uncomfortably aware of your every action, your every expression, and every single possible judgement that you may be emanating in those around you, whether this judgement is real or not. It’s also so difficult to distinguish why someone outside of this minority may be mistreating you - is it because they’re misogynist, or because they’re racist and bigoted, homophobic perhaps, or because they just don’t like me, or because I’m actually the one in the wrong? Couple the possibility of the last option with the lumbering shadow of imposter syndrome and you have a perfect pretty cocktail sitting ready to wreck your self esteem. Lovely.

A guy in my very technical and practical elective asked me today how the fuck I was competent enough to be an engineering student. I, in all my easygoing- and clearheadedness, and in all the confidence I had amassed in the past two years of my engineering degree with my exam results and my GPA, frankly did not know how to respond to that. Frankly because this is a question I ask myself everyday. At every moment of weakness where I don’t understand a problem or a concept, at every period of uselessness I feel in a project group because I don’t know how to contribute, in every break down where I myself question my competence and desperately to no avail try to reason with myself why I deserve to be here and how well I have done so far, it’s a question of who I think I am sitting across the same table as this mega-competent, smartass, all-knowing guy. And I know all the logical and rational reasons why I need to stop doubting my place here, but it’s really so difficult, especially when this mega-competent, smartass, all-knowing guy says out loud those same words that I’ve been secretly telling myself the entire time. Even worse when it comes from a conservative, chauvinistic guy who probably has all these prejudices of how incompetent, irrational, and emotional women are, giving any sort of emotional reaction to whatever he just said seems like a crime I am committing to the entirety of womanhood. It’s fucking exhausting, having to balance all these stupid considerations I have to take into account, against the overwhelming urge to slap him in the face, especially when he rubbed salt specifically in the wound where it fucking burns all the time anyways.

Someone with a champion mindset would steel themselves and use this as constructive feedback to develop themselves and grow stronger despite what these men say, but this is exactly what I had intended to do by choosing such a technical elective in which I had no past experience in and wanted to challenge myself and learn. It’s just fucked that I have to deal with this in my development arc. But then isn’t this just always going to be the case no matter what arc I am in? Is this actually what I signed up to when I decided to join the flanks of the ‘women in STEM’? Sometimes I just wish a girl much smarter and more competent than myself were in my place and were to prove dickheads like these wrong, and then I hate myself for wishing this and for thinking this about myself. It’s like I fear that I’m an imposter posing in the flanks of women that strive to prove these prejudices wrong, and I think this is exactly what stereotype threat is. The fear of being an imposter when you choose to be a minority in the field which packs so many little notions of hatred against your very people, and the dread that you’re proving these notions right and letting your people down. Sometimes I just want to quit this entire engineering thing because this is all so genuinely exhausting, but then I hate the feeling of proving them right by quitting, and so I continue and suffer, and so in the end these people get what they want anyways, that being my suffering. It’s fucked in every direction.

I wish it were as simple as letting this minority thing go and solely representing me as myself, and this would honestly be ideal in a perfect world, but it’s really difficult when these prejudices and stereotypes are very real things that affect us no matter how much more woke the world gets. Humans naturally distinguish themselves into in-groups and out-groups, and as long as that exists, minorities will always be perceived as minorities and representative of a bigger group than just themselves, and the only way you can escape from this is letting go of all external and societal expectations and living on the fringes of society pretty much. It’s a fucked state of affairs, and living in Europe as a brown female engineering student is quite the short end of the stick.
